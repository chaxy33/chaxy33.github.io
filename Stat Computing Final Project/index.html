What Drives Loan Interest Rates?

Author

Chase Witherspoon

We see that these two factors, age and loan purpose are weak factors in determining the loan interest rate. Now, let’s make our model to see which factors are the most important.

This model has the factors that are statistically significant. However since the dataset is so large (n=29,000), some of these factors are quite practically insignificant.

For example, we discovered person_age is a weak factor in determining the loan_int_rate, because it’s practically insignificant, but it’s a statistically significant factor.

\[ \begin{aligned} {\text{loan\_int\_rate}} &= 7.3674 - 0.0051(\text{person\_age}) - 0.00000018(\text{person\_income}) \\ &\quad + 0.3268(\text{OWNERSHIP\_OTHER}) + 0.1362(\text{OWNERSHIP\_OWN}) + 0.1127(\text{OWNERSHIP\_RENT}) \\ &\quad + 3.6334(\text{GradeB}) + 6.0855(\text{GradeC}) + 7.9699(\text{GradeD}) \\ &\quad + 9.6154(\text{GradeE}) + 11.1983(\text{GradeF}) + 12.8389(\text{GradeG}) \\ &\quad + 0.0000075(\text{loan\_amnt}) - 0.3371(\text{loan\_percent\_income}) + 0.0081(\text{cred\_hist\_length}) \end{aligned} \]

As you can see, the only genuine practically significant factor is the loan_grade.

If you move down the credit tiers from Grade A to Grade G the predicted interest rates are increased by 3.63% (from Grade A to Grade B) up to 12.84% (Grade A to Grade G) which essentially dominates the entire model.

You can see that the orange bars are statistically significant but not practically significant at all. The most statistically but not practically significant factor is Home Ownership, which is maxed out around 0.33%. This does not contribute a lot to the model.

If you were to take out a $15,000 loan for three years with a 7.33% interest rate vs a 7.00%, you would be paying around 80 more dollars, which isn’t too much of an increase.
