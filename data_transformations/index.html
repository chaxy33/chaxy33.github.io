# Explain via a manual computation, how finding cor(df$temp, df$chirps) , mean(df$temp) , sd(df$temp) , mean(df$chirps) , sd(df$chirps) results in the above linear regression equation as well

upd_cor <- cor(df2$temp, df2$chirps, use = "pairwise.complete.obs")
upd_cor
[1] 0.970876
sdtemp <- sd(df2$temp)
sdchirps <- sd(df2$chirps)
meantemp <- mean(df2$temp)
meanchirps <- mean(df2$chirps)

intercept <- upd_cor * (sdtemp/sdchirps)
slope <- meantemp - (intercept*meanchirps)

intercept
[1] 0.7139926
slope

# The intercept and slope is 0.714 and 46.474 respectively.

# You can compute this by finding the correlation between the temperature and chirps. From here, you can compute the standard deviation and mean of each vector. From this point on, you can find the intercept by multiplying the ratios of the standard deviations with the correlation.

# You can then find the slope by subtracting the intercept multiplied by the mean chirps and subtract that from the mean temperature.
